| No | Principle                                      | One-Line Meaning                                                                           |
| -- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1  | **Design for Reads, Not Relationships**        | Model data based on how the application reads it, not just how entities relate.            |
| 2  | **Design Around Access Patterns**              | Start with the APIs and queries your application needs most frequently.                    |
| 3  | **Keep Related Data Together (Data Locality)** | Data that is frequently accessed together should usually live together.                    |
| 4  | **Move Computation to Design Time**            | Design documents to reduce expensive joins and complex queries later.                      |
| 5  | **Optimize the Common Case**                   | Optimize for the operations that happen most often (usually reads).                        |
| 6  | **Accept Controlled Duplication**              | Duplicate small amounts of data when it significantly improves performance.                |
| 7  | **One Document = One Business Object**         | A document should represent a complete business entity or event whenever practical.        |
| 8  | **Balance Read vs Write Trade-offs**           | Faster reads may mean more work during writes, and vice versa. Choose based on workload.   |
| 9  | **Avoid Unnecessary Joins**                    | Prefer document structures that minimize `$lookup` operations when possible.               |
| 10 | **Think About Growth Early**                   | Design for future document size, collection growth, and scalability—not just today's data. |

Interview Questions ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
1. What is MongoDB's maximum document size?

16 MB per BSON document.

2. Why does MongoDB have a document size limit?

To keep documents manageable for storage, memory, networking, and replication.

3. Should we embed arrays?

Yes, if they are bounded and closely related to the parent document.

4. What is an unbounded array?

An array with no predictable upper limit, such as comments, followers, or messages.

5. Give examples of bounded arrays.
Product images
User addresses
Emergency contacts
Phone numbers
Certificates
6. Give examples of unbounded arrays.
Comments
Likes
Followers
Messages
Orders
Notifications

==================================================================================================


Interview Questions ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
1. What is referencing?

Storing the identifier of another document instead of embedding the document itself.

2. When should we use references?

When related data:

Grows without bound
Has its own lifecycle
Is shared across many parent documents
Is often accessed independently
3. Is referencing similar to SQL foreign keys?

Conceptually yes.

But MongoDB references do not automatically enforce referential integrity like SQL foreign keys.

4. Why are references more scalable?

Because documents remain smaller, and related data can grow independently.
